## Challenge

Many of these books dealt with ideas that are hard to capture in a single image. For each title, I started from the central idea of the text and looked for a way to give it visual form. Sometimes that meant building a metaphor; other times, working with typography, a photograph, or the book as an object.

I produced every cover myself, including the illustration and photography, without stock imagery or outside collaborators. The collection deliberately avoids a single house style.

## Guardians of the Kingdom

<p class="note">Crítica a los Guardianes del Reino</p>

A critical homage to *The Impossible Country of the Philosophers*, a foundational text on Costa Rican philosophy. The book questions the idealized way the country sees itself, an image that doesn’t hold up under scrutiny.

I used an egg in royal blue and gold as a metaphor: a small, ornate kingdom that looks perfect from the outside but is hollow and breaks easily.

<CaseImage src={guardians} alt="Cover of Crítica a los Guardianes del Reino: a broken eggshell in royal blue glitter with a gold lattice, on a white cover" />

## Six Years of Sex

<p class="note">Seis Años de Sexo</p>

A collection of six years of sex columns published in SoHo Costa Rica, told through three female protagonists.

I wanted the cover to keep the sensual tone of the columns without using the magazine’s usual image of the female body as an object of desire. I worked with red for skin and heat, and with an abstract composition that suggests female anatomy, with a small damp mark of pleasure. The image comes from the women’s point of view rather than from someone looking at them.

<CaseImage src={sixYears} alt="Cover of Seis Años de Sexo: folds of red satin forming an abstract composition" />

## The Reader’s Challenge

<p class="note">Los Desafíos del Lector</p>

An academic book on literary reading, built around the iceberg analogy: only a small part of a text’s meaning is visible on the surface.

I applied the same idea to the cover. Seen up close, it is a field of black dots; from a distance, the dots form an iceberg. The image needs some effort from the viewer, as the book says a text does.

<CaseImage src={readersChallenge} alt="Cover of Los Desafíos del Lector: a field of black dots of varying sizes that forms an iceberg when seen from a distance" />

## The Malady of Life

<p class="note">Mal de Vida</p>

Eleven stories that follow a human life from birth to death, moving between existential realism and the supernatural.

I designed the book as a narrative object: a radiant open eye on the front, and the same eye almost extinguished on the back, so that reading the book follows the same arc as the stories. Gold lines on black give it a ritual, nocturnal tone.

<CaseImage src={maladyOfLife} alt="Front and back of Mal de Vida: a radiant open eye drawn in gold lines on the front, and the same eye almost closed on the back, on black" />

## The Giant Island

<p class="note">La Isla Gigante</p>

A history of Cuban culture from 1959 to 2016. It covers almost six decades, and any single image risked tying the book to one period.

I avoided recognizable icons and worked with typography and landscape. The torn silhouette of the island identifies the subject, and the faded pastels and worn textures recall walls, sun, and the passing of time. These are things readers associate with Cuba in general rather than with a particular decade.

<CaseImage src={giantIsland} alt="Cover of La Isla Gigante: the title in large purple letters over the torn white silhouette of Cuba, on worn teal, yellow, and pink textures" />

## The Book as a Cultural Device

<p class="note">El Libro como Dispositivo Cultural</p>

A short essay arguing that a book is not just an object with pages and a spine, but a set of signs arranged to be read, an idea that digitization is now putting into question.

I reduced the cover to the most basic unit of written language, the letter a, repeated and nested in a serif typeface at different scales. The result is itself a set of signs organized to be read.

<CaseImage src={culturalDevice} alt="Cover of El Libro como Dispositivo Cultural: the letter a in a black serif typeface, repeated and nested at decreasing scales" />

## Life Elsewhere

<p class="note">La Vida en Otra Parte</p>

An essay on migration in Costa Rica, written from the idea that migrants bring aspirations, families, and plans for the future, not only labor.

The cover is about the family behind the migrant worker. A torn photograph taped back together stands for a family divided by migration, and for the effort to hold it together anyway.

<CaseImage src={lifeElsewhere} alt="Cover of La Vida en Otra Parte: a torn photograph of a family, shown as white silhouettes, taped back together" />

## The Neoliberal Religion of the Market and Human Rights

<p class="note">La Religión Neoliberal del Mercado y los Derechos Humanos</p>

The book argues that neoliberalism works like a secular religion: the market takes the place theology once had, and people end up serving the market instead of the other way around.

I started from Michelangelo’s *Creation of Adam* as a reference to religion. Drawn in line, the gesture also recalls Adam Smith’s “invisible hand,” and the neon treatment brings in commerce and advertising. I placed the hands so they suggest submission rather than an encounter.

<CaseImage src={neoliberalReligion} alt="Cover of La Religión Neoliberal del Mercado y los Derechos Humanos: the hands from the Creation of Adam drawn in pink neon around the title in a blue neon circle, on black" />
