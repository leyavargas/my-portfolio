## Context and Problem

DiDi is a Chinese ride-hailing company expanding across Latin America, with communication needs that differed significantly from its home market.

The visual illustration system inherited from headquarters was too generic to build brand credibility in the region and didn’t address marketing communication needs.

<CaseImage src={legacy} alt="Grid of the generic illustrations inherited from headquarters: business avatars, standing figures and car scenes" />

With one design team in Mexico and another supporting eight Spanish-speaking markets, the lack of unified visual direction led to fragmented communications, longer production times, and an inconsistent regional brand presence.

<CaseImage src={mexicoLatam} alt="Illustrations made by the Mexico team next to those made by the rest of Latam, showing two different visual styles" />

<CaseImage src={byCountry} alt="In-app communications from DiDi Mexico, Dominican Republic, Chile and Costa Rica, each with a different layout and style" />

## Solutions

The challenge was to build a scalable visual infrastructure that could adapt to regional needs within significant technical constraints. The solution was two interconnected systems:

1. **Illustration System:** A modular visual library built for regional representation and recurring communication needs.
2. **Digital Communication Guidelines System:** A standardized framework and reusable asset library designed to make communications faster and more consistent.

As the Digital Communications Sr. Designer for Latam, I led the design, implementation, and ongoing development of both systems.

## 01 - The Illustration System

Built as a modular, vector-based system, the illustrations could quickly adapt to different themes, audiences, and contexts while keeping digital assets lightweight. The visual style evolved DiDi’s existing brand language rather than replacing it.

Designers could select and combine pre-built components from a centralized Illustrator library, while non-design teams accessed curated PNG assets through Confluence, reducing the need to create or request assets from scratch.

## 02 - Digital Communication Guidelines System

A reusable framework for producing consistent communications across markets, channels, and audiences. Designed for both drivers and passengers (with drivers representing approximately 75–80% of communication needs) the system prioritized accessibility and scalability within existing software and licensing constraints.

<CaseImage src={audiences} alt="Communication formats for the driver side, both audiences and the passenger side: Xpanel, pop up, email or landing page, ops card and ops card plus" />

### Design Decisions

This was not a rebrand; the system extended DiDi’s existing identity into a scalable communication framework. I identified the recurring communication needs such as tables, buttons, and icons, and translated them into a set of reusable components and rules.

<CaseImage src={decisions} alt="DiDi brand colors, Apira typeface weights, structure and sizing on a 750px grid, typographic hierarchy and text contrast ratio" />

<CaseImage src={components} alt="Components: primary, secondary and terms buttons, icons for buttons, contrast ratios, highlights, tables and previous button styles" />

### Built for Faster Production

I created ready-to-use components, templates, and a UI gallery of frequently used app screens, allowing designers to quickly reuse and combine assets across communications.

Given software, licensing, and budget constraints, components were created in Adobe Illustrator and manually uploaded to an internal tool.

## Testing

To validate the system, I worked with the Operations team to survey drivers, the primary audience, testing key elements such as phone sizing, legibility, and overall communication clarity.

I also conducted a heuristic evaluation based on usability best practices, comparing the previous and updated communications. Result: +20% improvement in overall UX score.

## Impact of Both Systems

The two systems were adopted across the LATAM team in 8 countries: Dominican Republic, Panama, Costa Rica, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Chile, and Argentina, improving consistency and making digital communications faster and easier to produce.
