## A Fragmented Region

DiDi is a Chinese ride-hailing company expanding across Latin America, with communication needs that differed significantly from its home market.

The visual illustration system inherited from headquarters was too generic to build brand credibility in the region and didn’t address marketing communication needs.

<CaseImage src={legacy} alt="Grid of the generic illustrations inherited from headquarters: business avatars, standing figures and car scenes" />

With one design team in Mexico and another supporting eight Spanish-speaking markets, the lack of unified visual direction led to fragmented communications, longer production times, and an inconsistent regional brand presence.

<CaseImage src={mexicoLatam} alt="Illustrations made by the Mexico team next to those made by the rest of LATAM, showing two different visual styles" />

<CaseImage src={byCountry} alt="In-app communications from DiDi Mexico, Dominican Republic, Chile and Costa Rica, each with a different layout and style" />

## Solutions

The challenge was to build a scalable visual infrastructure that could adapt to regional needs within significant technical constraints. The solution was two interconnected systems:

1. **Illustration System:** A modular visual library built for regional representation and recurring communication needs.
2. **Digital Communication Guidelines System:** A standardized framework and reusable asset library designed to make communications faster and more consistent.

As Senior Digital Communications Designer for the LATAM team, I led the design, implementation, and ongoing development of both systems, with the support of Cristina Jaramillo, Design Manager.

<CaseImage src={beforeAfter} alt="Animation comparing communications before and after the system in Ecuador, Colombia, Costa Rica and Peru" />

## Constraints & Trade-Offs

- **Interim, not definitive:** with HQ’s new illustration system on the way but no timeline, I evolved the existing style instead of creating a new one.
- **Illustration over photography:** with no stock imagery available, illustration was the main resource. It was faster to customize than retouched photography and kept files lighter.
- **Existing tools over ideal tools:** with no budget for new licenses, the system was built in Illustrator and then exported into DiDi’s in-house tools.
- **Drivers first approach:** drivers represented 75–80% of communication needs, so the system prioritized their formats.

## 01 — The Illustration System

### Evolve, Don’t Reinvent

After over three months exploring and testing new illustration styles with the Mexico team, the project was paused pending HQ’s new illustration system. With no timeline and inconsistencies growing across markets, my manager and I decided to refine DiDi’s existing illustration language, improving quality and consistency while staying true to the current brand and HQ’s future direction.

<CaseImage src={evolveTimeline} alt="Refined DiDi illustrations of diverse characters above a project timeline from January to April: first meetings, finding references, first sketches, moodboards creation, final review and A/B test" />

### Modular by Design

Built as a modular, vector-based system, the illustrations could quickly adapt to different themes, audiences, and contexts while keeping digital assets lightweight.

<CaseImage src={construction} alt="Illustration construction rules: face features from front, side and 3/4 views, body proportions, brand colors, skin tone combinations and hand variations" />

<CaseImage src={avatars} alt="Grid of diverse character avatars with different hairstyles and skin tones" />

### Designed Around Communication Needs

The inherited system lacked illustrations for recurring marketing needs such as discounts, safety features, and seasonal campaigns, as well as DiDi’s expanding services like motorcycle rides and food delivery. I designed the new library to support these evolving needs.

### Built for Faster Production

Designers could select and combine pre-built components from a centralized Illustrator library, while non-design teams accessed curated PNG assets through Confluence, reducing the need to create or request assets from scratch.

## 02 — Communication Guidelines

A reusable framework for producing consistent communications across markets, channels, and audiences. Designed for both drivers and passengers, the system prioritized accessibility and scalability.

<CaseImage src={audiences} alt="Communication formats for the driver side, both audiences and the passenger side: Xpanel, pop up, email or landing page, ops card and ops card plus" />

### Design Decisions

Like the illustration system, the guidelines extended DiDi’s existing identity rather than replacing it. I identified recurring needs, such as tables, buttons, and icons, and turned them into reusable components and rules.

<CaseImage src={decisions} alt="DiDi brand colors, Apira typeface weights, structure and sizing on a 750px grid, typographic hierarchy and text contrast ratio" />

<CaseImage src={components} alt="Components: primary, secondary and terms buttons, icons for buttons, contrast ratios, highlights, tables and previous button styles" />

### Built for Faster Production

I created ready-to-use components, templates, and a UI gallery of frequently used app screens, allowing designers to quickly reuse and combine assets across communications.

### Testing

To validate the system, I worked with the Operations team to survey drivers, the primary audience, testing key elements such as phone sizing, legibility, and overall communication clarity.

## The Systems in Use

The systems scaled beyond their six core formats: they were adapted for WhatsApp messages, used by an external agency for social media, and applied to print, including a mural proposal for the Costa Rica and Mexico offices.

## Impact of Both Systems

The two systems were adopted across the LATAM team in 8 countries: Dominican Republic, Panama, Costa Rica, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Chile, and Argentina, improving brand consistency and making digital communications faster and easier to produce.

Measuring the results:

- **45% faster delivery:** reduction in average ticket turnaround time, measured in Airtable before and after implementation.
- **+20% UX score:** improvement based on a heuristic evaluation of previous vs. updated communication.
- **Design team NPS:** increased from 75% to 100%, based on internal team surveys in Q4 2020 and Q4 2021.
